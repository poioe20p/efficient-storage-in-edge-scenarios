## 1. Objective

When a newly provisioned backend becomes ready, how do the three admission rules — admit on the readiness message alone (A `event_only`), the message with a bounded re-check (B `hybrid`), or a periodic check that ignores the message (C `reconcile`; 10 s poll) — determine **whether and when the new capacity starts serving requests**, and **what users experience**, under an **intact** readiness path, **total readiness-message loss**, and a **claim that is true too early** (readiness announced 10 s before the backend can serve)? This campaign asks no new question: it **completes the family's evidence to the thesis-wide seven-replicate standard** — seven seed-matched blocks for intact, lost and premature (with a matched no-fault partner per premature block) and seven runs for each intact-source supporting set — via ≈35 new runs (38 executed in C1–C3, including quarantined-and-replaced attempts; the 22-run C4 wake completion brings the campaign total to 60 — §3.1). It is the completion pillar of the reframed RQ3 (readiness-admission coordination → usable-capacity realization; `tese/research_questions/rq3/rq3_reframing_recommendation.md`).

Independent variable: admission semantics (A/B/C) crossed with readiness-channel condition (intact / lost / premature at the frozen 10 s lead). Hypothesis: the certified Family-4/5 directions hold at n = 7 — under intact, A and B admit earlier than C by roughly C's check period; under lost, A degrades visibly but boundedly while B and C recover; under premature, A and B absorb damage against their seed-matched no-fault partners while C stays near-immune.

### 3.4 Cross-run trends

- **The rule ordering is the campaign's most stable result**: C ≤ 2 pp damage in 7/7 premature pairs and A/B > C in 7/7 same-fault blocks (E-P2); A shows the largest damage (median +14.38 pp, every usable pair positive).
