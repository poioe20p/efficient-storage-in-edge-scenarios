Dumbed down rq:
"If the controller sees demand late, or misses the intermediate demand evidence entirely,
 its scale-out decisions are worse — but which failure, delay or missing evidence, actually hurts?"

Why:
- Essentially acroos the SOTA no papers distinguish and or highlit the effects of delivery old info
against delivering some parts of the info.

So more detailed we evaluate 3 modes:
- Event-preserving reference (A: ep) — every completed telemetry window delivered exactly once, in source order (fresh + complete).
- Delayed event-preserving (B: delayed) — same ordered windows, fixed pre-registered delay (+30 s), no burst replay (stale + complete).
- Latest-state polling (C: ls, poll-30) — consumer gets only the most recent completed window; intermediate windows are not delivered (fresh + lossy, ~1/3).
- Sampled-push (D: sp, /3) — periodic sampling of the window stream (fresh + lossy, ~1/3).

Looks good, allows us to know:
- Allows us to understand each one's impact on user service quality and scaling efficiency.
- Gives us 2 different ways of telemetry delivery and links to the scaling decision module and 
evaluates that interface by varying the source.

In terms of delivery-semantics machinery:
- We have a script that allows us to guarantee event comms in the exact ways we
want.
